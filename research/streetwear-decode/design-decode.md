## Market context
- Indian streetwear crossed ~US$1.5B in 2025; 800+ digital-first brands launched since 2019.
- Dominant 2025–26 trends: structured oversized (220–280 GSM), acid/stone wash, refined cargos + parachute pants, **Neo-Desi Futurism** (mythology, Devanagari, craft techniques on technical silhouettes), drop-scarcity culture, hip-hop co-sign economy.
- Roxylane is the proof-of-concept for Matsya's exact price band: process-led tees at ₹1,920–2,500 selling out drop after drop with only ~17K Instagram followers.

# PART 2 — The Decoded Design System (what's actually working)

## T-shirts: 8 rules

**1. Oversized/boxy at 230–280 GSM is the entry ticket, not a differentiator.**
100% cotton heavy jersey or French terry, drop shoulders, unisex sizing. 260–280 GSM is the premium sweet spot (Bluorng 260, Jaywalking/Chapter 2 280). Urban Monkey's collector ceiling is 380 GSM acid-washed fleece. Below 230 GSM reads mass-market.

**2. Texture beats print — "process" is the new graphic.**
The highest sell-through is wash/feel-led, not graphic-led. Roxylane's signature move: garment-dye black → apply emblem → acid-wash the whole garment so the logo survives as a tone-on-tone reveal — *"not printed but earned through process."* 17 of 19 tee SKUs sold out. Almost Gods: stonewash, "each piece is unique." Chapter 2: double bio-silicone wash. Flat DTG printing is dead at this tier.

**3. Embellishment is the margin ladder (2–3x from plain to top).**
Plain → screen/puff graphic (+30–50%) → wash/distress (+15–25%) → embroidery/jacquard/crystal/all-over (2–2.5x plain). Bluorng: ₹3,700 → 4,500 → 6,300 → 8,900. Almost Gods: ₹3,375 → 5,500 → 6,500 → 10,500 (aari hand embroidery — Indian craft as the premium lever).

**4. One ownable word/symbol, repeated forever.**
Almost Gods: gothic blackletter + Alpha Cross. Chapter 2: "Anti-Everything / Un-herd." Roxylane: Stone®/Concrete material vocabulary (trademarked product names as IP). Bluorng: fauna. Jaywalking: the badly-scanned logo. Typography splits by tier — blackletter (art-lux), grotesque slogan caps (mid-premium), hand-scrawl (drop culture).

**5. Front + back double hit is standard; the back is the hero.**
Small chest hit + big back statement in nearly every product description. All-over prints reserved for top-price pieces (Jaywalking ₹9,500 full-sleeves).

**6. Palette: washed black + bone + earth minerals.**
Coal, slate, sand, clay, mocha, sage, rust everywhere. Brights only as one signature accent (Bluorng orange, Roxylane's orange label). Color *names* are marketing copy: "Coal Wash," "Dune," "Sandstone."

**7. Scarcity is a design feature.**
Small drops (~10 live tees at Jaywalking), numbered editions (UM Legacy 003), "sold out" as social proof, no-restock rules (Bluorng). Deep-inventory brands compensate with lower prices — availability itself signals tier.

**8. The differentiation frontier is desi.**
Aari embroidery, jacquard, Garuda/Asura mythology (Urban Monkey artist collabs), Devanagari text, "Brown Munde" crossovers. Simultaneously a post-graphic counter-current (Warping Theories: zero graphics, pure texture knits) pulls the older premium buyer. The open lane: **mythology executed through process, not through printed clip-art.**

## Pants: 7 rules

**1. Heavy-washed baggy denim is the anchor SKU at every tier.**
Wide leg, 5-pocket, contrast stitch, back-pocket embroidery, 12–14 oz cotton. The differentiator is the wash name: washed black > acid/brushed > ombre/spot/spray > stone. Sellout proof: Bluorng's entire ₹11,995 wash line, Chapter 2 ombre, Urban Monkey Rivet Denim. Price scales cleanly by tier: ₹2.6k (Six5Six) → ₹3–4.2k (Chapter 2) → ₹7k (Warping) → ₹9–12k (Bluorng).

**2. Pocket-count inflation — the number IS the spec.**
6, 9, 11 (Roxylane), 12 (Urban Monkey), 14 + 2 detachable (Bluorng). Roxylane's clever hybrid: an 11-pocket utility pant cut from **380 GSM French terry** — knit comfort, utility look, ₹4,750, selling through.

**3. Raw denim is the new premium story.** Bluorng's 2026 bet: 420 GSM non-prewashed raw denim, "evolves with wear." Patina narrative justifies the ladder top.

**4. Pleats are the premiumization lever.** Curved pleats stitched from the hem (Jaywalking), pleated cords (Warping), even a pleated sweatpant (Almost Gods). Streetwear-tailoring hybrids price 1.3–1.5x baseline.

**5. Parachute = the entry to "technical."** Crushed 200–210 GSM poly/nylon, 3D pentagon pockets, snap adjusters, knee darts, drawcord waist. Mid-ladder everywhere (UM ₹2,000 → Warping ₹5,990).

**6. Heavyweight knit bottoms with published GSM.** 320–380 GSM is the credibility number. Elastic waist + drawcord below ₹6k; button/zip fly for denim above.

**7. Deconstruction sells the ladder top.** "Badly Stitched" intentional imperfection, painted pants (sold out at Almost Gods), one-of-ones. Paired with scarcity mechanics ("100 units only," made-to-order).

**Color frequency across all nine catalogs:** washed black → ecru/sand → olive → mocha/brown (the riser) → washed indigo → grey. **Open spaces spotted:** no true double-waistbands anywhere; indigo is underexploited outside 11.11's ₹27k craft tier; jorts are now a real category at every tier.

# PART 3 — The MATSYA Design System

*Matsya: the first avatar — the fish that carried knowledge through the flood. The entire system is built on one idea:* **water as process.** *Where Roxylane owns concrete/stone, Matsya owns the deluge. Every competitor is doing acid wash as an effect; Matsya makes the wash the mythology.*

## 3.1 Brand codes

| Element | System |
|---|---|
| **Core metaphor** | The Deluge. Garments are "survivors of the flood" — every wash, fade, and salt-line is narrative, not decoration |
| **Wordmark** | MTSYA (vowel-dropped, as built on Shopify) in an engraved/etched serif — between Almost Gods' blackletter and Roxylane's industrial sans. Secondary mark: मत्स्य in Devanagari, used small (nape, hem, pocket) |
| **Symbol** | The Scale — a single fish-scale geometric unit (overlapping crescents). Tessellates into patterns (jacquard, embroidery, tonal print); one scale alone works as a chest hit. This is the Alpha Cross / Gorilla equivalent: one shape, repeated forever |
| **Accent color** | **Deep indigo** — the ownable brand color (Bluorng = orange, Roxylane = orange label, Matsya = indigo). Ties to water AND to India's oldest dye tradition; nobody in the ₹2k band uses it |
| **Label detail** | Woven indigo label on rear/hem with drop number: "DELUGE 01 — 1/150" |

## 3.3 T-shirt system (₹1,900–2,499 band, per your pricing strategy)

**Base garment (non-negotiable spec):** 240–260 GSM 100% cotton heavy jersey, boxy fit, drop shoulder, ribbed collar, unisex XS–XXL. Publish the GSM everywhere — it's the credibility number.

**Signature process — "The Tide Wash":** garment-dye → apply tonal Scale emblem or MTSYA mark → salt/acid wash the finished garment so the mark emerges as a ghost — *earned, not printed* (Roxylane's proven mechanic, re-authored through water instead of stone). Each piece washes differently = "no two survived the flood the same."

**Placement grid:** left-chest single Scale (small) + full back hero (large) — the standard double hit. मत्स्य at back nape. No front-dominant graphics.

**Price ladder within the band:**
| Tier | Price | Construction |
|---|---|---|
| Ghat Tee (essential) | ₹1,900 | Tide-washed solid, tonal embroidered Scale at chest, no back print |
| Tide Tee (core graphic) | ₹2,150 | Tide Wash reveal mark + back hero graphic (puff or high-density) |
| Deluge Tee (drop hero) | ₹2,499 | Heavier wash story (ombre depth-fade: dark hem → light shoulder, like rising water) + embroidered detail |

**Motif direction (the open lane — mythology through process, never clip-art):** wave geometry from temple stone carvings, the Scale tessellation, flood-line marks (a horizontal fade line across the chest like a waterline), Devanagari used typographically not decoratively. NOT: literal fish illustrations, god imagery, anything that reads as calendar art.

## 3.5 Drop system

- **Named, numbered drops:** DELUGE 01, 02… (Roxylane's RX Construction model). 100–150 units per SKU, never restocked (Bluorng's rule — scarcity as brand).
- **Drop composition:** 3 tees + 1 bottom + 1 accent piece. Tight catalog (~8 live SKUs) reads premium; sold-out archive stays visible on site as social proof.
- **Deadstock fabric angle** (your sourcing strategy) becomes the story: limited because the fabric itself is finite — "when the bolt ends, the piece ends." This out-authenticates artificial scarcity.
- **Sequence with your existing plan:** wedge (Tide Tee at ₹2,150) → then base pieces.

## 3.6 Positioning sentence

> **Matsya makes flood-washed heavyweight streetwear — Indian mythology told through process, not print. Every piece survives a wash no two garments emerge from the same, in drops that end when the fabric does.**

Direct comp: Roxylane (same price, same process-led mechanic, proven sellouts) — but Matsya adds the thing Roxylane lacks: a mythology, an ownable color (indigo), and a symbol system that can scale to embroidery, jacquard, and craft collabs (aari embroidery is the proven path from ₹2.5k to ₹5k+ later).
